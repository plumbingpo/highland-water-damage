Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Highland

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

This water is not clean. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Keep Out of the BasementDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceSee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallTake pictures of the pump and the water level, bring valuables that are still dry upstairs, and phone us. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Outside the house, a crushed, clogged or separated discharge pipe.
  • Gravel, silt or debris in the pit jamming the impeller or clogging the intake.
  • Stuck or snagged float switches, which never tell the pump to start.
  • Loss of power at the pump alone, from a loose cord or a tripped circuit.
  • Check valve trouble, which makes the pump move the same water again and again.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Outlets and Wiring

Basement outlets sit low on the wall, where rising water can reach them. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

Drywall and Wall Insulation

Dirty water climbs from the slab into insulation and drywall, far higher than the line you can see. Because the water is contaminated, the wet lower section is cut out, not dried.

Carpet and Flooring

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Vinyl plank and laminate floors generally have to be pulled as well, since dirty water stays under them.

Bottom Plates and Studs

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. With the wall opened up, this wood is scrubbed, treated and then dried to normal readings.
