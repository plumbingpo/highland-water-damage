Hardwood Floor Water Damage in Highland

Wood floors react to water quickly. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. Cupping alone is no reason to give up on the floor. Boards of solid wood often return to flat if they are dried during those early days. What matters is removing moisture from the wood and from its subfloor steadily and under control.

We dry hardwood with floor drying mats. The mats are sealed to the surface and connected to a vacuum unit that draws moisture up through the boards and seams, while dehumidifiers keep the room air dry. Expect it to run a week or more, which is longer than drywall or carpet needs. Sanding has to wait until the wood is fully dry. Sand a cupped floor too soon and it will crown later.

What It Looks Like

  • Gaps opening between boards after a floor has dried too fast
  • A washboard feel underfoot from edges that stand taller than board centers
  • A hazy, whitened or flaking finish where the water sat
  • New squeaks or popping sounds when you walk across the wet area
  • Sections of flooring that have risen, forming a tent shape along a seam
  • Planks with a rounded hump down the center and lower sides
How We Fix It

Our Hardwood Floor Water Damage Process

1

Take Baseline Readings

We take readings on the boards, the subfloor and unaffected flooring nearby for comparison. From those numbers we learn the depth of the water and if the floor can realistically be saved.

2

Extract Standing Water

Standing water is extracted right away and rugs and furniture are moved off the wet area. Boards swell less when the seams stop taking in water sooner.

3

Install the Mat System

A vacuum system hooked to mats sealed on the surface lifts moisture out of the boards from above. To avoid cracking, dehumidifiers keep conditions steady and the wood dries at an even rate.

4

Track Progress and Report

Readings are logged every one to two days and the equipment stays until the wet zone equals the dry comparison areas. Then we tell you whether the floor needs refinishing, a few boards replaced, or nothing.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Sanding Too Early

Sanding cupped boards flat while they are still wet removes the raised edges. Once drying is complete, the middles sit higher and you have a crowned floor.

Damage Beyond the Room

Once below the boards, water moves. Cabinets and wall plates do not stop it, so it may enter adjacent rooms or come out at the ceiling one floor down.

Drying Too Fast

When the top of the wood dries much quicker than the inside, stress builds in the board. Lasting gaps and split or cracked boards are the usual outcome.

Wet Subfloor

The subfloor can hold moisture long after the boards feel dry. If it is not dried too, the floor will cup again or grow mold from underneath.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Water slips through the seams and spreads between the boards and subfloor. The surface may wipe dry while the underside stays soaked.

24 to 48 hours

Cupping Starts

The edges come up as each board expands. You might notice haze in the topcoat. Growth is able to begin below, on raw wood and on the subfloor panels.

Day 3 to 7

Boards Start Lifting

Pressure grows through the whole floor as the wood expands. Boards crush against each other, fasteners loosen and sections can begin to lift off the subfloor.

Past one week

Permanent Damage

Black stains, buckled boards and mold beneath the flooring are now probable. At this stage most of the wet area needs to be replaced rather than dried.
